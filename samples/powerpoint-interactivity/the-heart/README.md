## Prompt A

Create an interactive, full-bleed slide: a draggable outside/inside anatomical reveal of a human heart.

Imagery (AI-generated medical visualization, not vector art): a highly realistic 3D human heart, isolated with transparency, viewed slightly from the front so the major vessels and overall shape are clearly visible; and a subtle dark clinical background with soft depth and no other subject.

Composition: edge to edge, no margins. Place one large heart prominently in the center of the slide.

Add one vertical divider with a small circular drag handle; dragging the divider smoothly reveals the two synchronized views of the exact same heart.

Left = External View: the complete photorealistic exterior of the heart, including the major vessels, surface texture, coronary vessels, and natural anatomical coloring.

Right = Internal View: the same heart, identical size, rotation, position, lighting, and animation, but shown as a realistic anatomical cutaway revealing the internal structures: left and right atria, left and right ventricles, interventricular septum, mitral valve, tricuspid valve, aortic valve, pulmonary valve, chordae tendineae, and major vessel openings.

The cutaway should feel like the outer wall has been cleanly removed rather than showing a separate illustration.

The divider must act like an anatomical X-ray reveal: as it moves across the heart, the viewer transitions seamlessly from the intact exterior to the internal cutaway.

Structures on both sides must remain perfectly aligned across the divider.

Caption at the bottom of the slide: "◀ Drag to explore inside the heart ▶".

Animation: the heart beats continuously and realistically at approximately 65–75 beats per minute.

Use a subtle contraction-and-relaxation cycle rather than exaggerated scaling.

The ventricles should visibly contract during systole and relax during diastole, with a slight natural movement of the atria and major vessels.

Both the external and internal versions must use exactly the same animation values and timing so they remain perfectly synchronized across the draggable divider.

For the Internal View, animate the chamber walls and valves subtly with the heartbeat.

During ventricular contraction, show the atrioventricular valves closing and the aortic and pulmonary valves opening; during relaxation, reverse the motion.

Keep the movement medically plausible but visually understandable.

Optional subtle blood-flow visualization on the Internal View: use small semi-transparent moving particles or soft flowing highlights.

Use blue for deoxygenated blood entering the right atrium, moving through the right ventricle, and exiting through the pulmonary artery.

Use red for oxygenated blood entering through the pulmonary veins, moving through the left atrium and left ventricle, and exiting through the aorta.

Keep this visualization secondary to the anatomy.

Interaction: the user must be able to drag the divider freely left and right to inspect any portion of the outside versus inside anatomy.

The heart continues beating while the divider is dragged.

The interaction should feel smooth and immediate.

Text: heading "Inside the Human Heart" with "Human Heart" emphasized.

Small labels "External Anatomy" on the left and "Internal Anatomy" on the right.

Do not add additional explanatory paragraphs or clutter.

Visual style: sophisticated medical visualization suitable for a high-school, university, or professional presentation.

Photorealistic rather than cartoon-like.

Avoid excessive gore, blood, surgical imagery, or graphic tissue.

The cutaway should resemble a premium interactive anatomy application.

Lighting: soft studio-style lighting with enough directional light to reveal the heart's three-dimensional form.

Preserve identical lighting between the external and internal renderings.

Responsiveness: scale to any slide size without clipping or scrollbars.

Keep the heart centered and ensure the full heart and major vessels remain visible at common 16:9 slide dimensions.

Performance: optimize the interactive asset for smooth animation and dragging.

Use compressed WebP or similarly efficient image assets where possible.

Keep the total payload as small as practical while retaining enough anatomical detail for a large-screen presentation.

Static preview: create a real rendered preview of the finished visual, with the divider positioned approximately 50% across the heart so the viewer immediately sees half external anatomy and half internal anatomy.

Do not use a blank placeholder.
